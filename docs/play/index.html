Say a sentence.
It guesses which accent it heard.

Four seconds of speech, measured in your browser. It names an accent and the region that accent is usually heard in — and it tells you how sure it is, which is usually not very.

waiting for the microphone

Read the sentence out loud, in your normal voice, about a hand-span from the microphone. Or drop in a clip — it is decoded here too.

The sentence

I asked her to bring a bottle of water from the corner shop.

A fixed sentence makes samples comparable — the same words, the same vowel set, so what is left is how you say them.

This names an accent, not a person. It is not a guess at nationality, ethnicity or identity — an accent is something you learned from the people around you, and people move. A wrong answer here is the normal case, not a failure.

what happens to your audio

It stays here, and then it is gone.

The recording is decoded in this page, turned into eight numbers, and the audio is dropped. Nothing is uploaded, because there is nowhere to upload it to: the page has a Content-Security-Policy that forbids every network connection (connect-src 'none'), so a fetch or a WebSocket would be blocked by the browser itself rather than by a promise in the footer.

leaves device
Never. No server, no model download, no analytics.
stored
Nothing. The sample is discarded the moment the guess is made.
account
None, and no cookie that identifies you.
kept locally
Your theme choice, and the challenge scoreboard. Both live in this browser and both can be cleared from it.
the model
Ships inside this file. There is no download, so it also works with the network switched off.

The honest caveat: the eight measurements are real, but the accent profiles they are compared against are hand-set starting values, not values measured from a labelled corpus. The repository says exactly what that means and what it would take to fix it.
